## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** About as far from a generic daily-puzzle template as this genre gets. The diner metaphor isn't a skin — it's load-bearing through the whole stack: the result screen is a `GuestCheckHead` with a check number and "Orders 4 of 6," the guess budget is a punched card instead of a progress bar, misses are clue tickets torn off a printer with a dot-matrix animation, the order box says "Order a dish…," pending states say "asking the kitchen…" / "asking the bar…," and the footer reads "Best food in town! · No substitutions on the Special · Ask about our pie." After Dark renames every noun rather than reskinning (coaster/tab/Libations), and the share grid swaps exactly one glyph (⬜→⬛) so a mixed day/night post stays legible with no second legend. A generic Wordle clone could not absorb this UI unchanged. The one place genericness creeps in is After Dark's onboarding, which inherits the daily's components correctly but teaches nothing about what changed.

**Deterministic scan:** 3 findings total, all in `src/styles/game.css`, zero in `src/game`, `src/styles/base.css`, `src/audio`, `src/discord` — a genuinely clean result confirmed against a synthetic `.tsx` probe, so it's not an unscanned-file gap. `border-accent-on-rounded` (×2, the clue-ticket's colored top band and the notice modal's mustard band) and `side-tab` (×1, the six status-bar variants' colored left edge). All three read as false positives on inspection: each sits beside a code comment explicitly justifying the colored border as a deliberate, single-purpose signal — the notice modal's band is its *only* ornament and the thing that lets players tell it apart from every other modal at a glance; the status-bar edge is a documented fast-channel-plus-text-tag pair, which is exactly what the project's own accessibility rule requires. Worth a human glance, but not worth acting on — and catching a "thick colored border" pattern here that turns out to be three independently-justified, intentional choices is itself evidence for the specificity verdict rather than against it. One detector-side finding worth flagging directly: exit code stayed `0` on all three findings even though they're primary (not advisory) — reproduced on a minimal synthetic repro too, so don't trust exit code alone from this detector build; read the JSON finding count.

**Visual overlays:** None — browser injection wasn't attempted this run. No overlay is live in a `[Human]` tab.

## Overall Impression

A well-crafted, unusually specific piece of interaction design for a solo hobby project — the coach-mark system alone is better UX engineering than most funded products ship. The daily loop (guess → feedback → clue ticket → win/loss → receipt) is tight, accessible by construction, and emotionally paced correctly at both ends. The single biggest opportunity is that After Dark — a whole second mode gated behind finishing lunch — got the mechanic and the theme but none of the teaching that makes the daily approachable, which is a real gap given it's the newer, less-familiar half of the product.

## What's Working

1. **The coach-mark system is derived from round state, never a step counter or timer** (`shared/coach.ts`). A reload mid-first-round always lands on the correct beat, nothing can desync from a missed animation, and it's dismissible instantly by any pointer/keyboard/focus event — so it never punishes a returning player for looking like a beginner. This replaced a documented worse pattern (a rules modal shown before the player had seen a board).
2. **The BarBand's restraint is a real product-principle win, not just a nice detail.** It never auto-navigates, fades in a full beat after the check settles specifically so it can't interrupt someone reading their result, and its flare lights edges rather than sweeping across text — a change driven by a measured contrast regression (4.85 → 3.68) the team caught and fixed. The `soon` state is a sentence, not a disabled button, avoiding a documented anti-pattern on purpose.
3. **Accessibility redundancy is systemic, not spot-fixed.** Every attribute tile carries three independent channels (fill color, an `aria-hidden` glyph, `.sr-only` text) fed from one shared table, so they can't drift apart — and the codebase has a track record of catching and naming its own contrast regressions (a token reused in the wrong direction, twice) rather than papering over them.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] After Dark has no onboarding of any kind.**
- **Why it matters**: A player reaches the bar only after finishing lunch, so they're not brand-new to the *game* — but they are brand-new to a tile set that silently swaps Course/Protein for Spirit/Profile, and nothing on screen explains that "no spirit" is a real match value rather than missing data (that fact exists only in a source comment). The daily's whole onboarding philosophy — teach through the actual round, never a wall of text — simply isn't applied to the second mode.
- **Fix**: A single static legend line reusing `shared/announce.ts`'s existing wording, or a one-time coach mark gated on a new "first Nightcap" flag, shown once.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable onboard`

**[P1] The finished-daily toolbar can show 6 simultaneous controls on a 375px card.**
- **Why it matters**: How-to, My Stats, Leftovers, Your Check, After Dark, and Sound all compete on one row; the project's own CSS comment already acknowledges it no longer fits and falls back to wrapping. That's well past the ≤4-item working-memory guideline for a control a returning player scans every day.
- **Fix**: Fold the how-to link under a single overflow affordance, or move Sound out of the horizontal toolbar entirely (e.g., beside the build-tag footer, where it has no time pressure).
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable distill`

**[P2] The last guess gets no distinct emotional treatment.**
- **Why it matters**: `PunchCard` renders "1 guess left" with the same color, weight, and sound as "5 guesses left." This genre is built around exactly that tension point, and right now it's the one under-designed beat in an otherwise carefully paced emotional arc.
- **Fix**: A color or copy shift on the final punch (e.g., "Last order," a mustard/cherry tint), gated the same way `lastcall-bar` already is for After Dark's final hour.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable delight`

**[P2] A failed guess breaks voice with generic error copy.**
- **Why it matters**: `KitchenClosed` has carefully diner-voiced, actionable copy for a failed initial load, but a mid-round network hiccup in `submitGuess`'s catch block surfaces the raw `error.message` in a plain `.error-note` — no personality, no next step.
- **Fix**: Route guess-submission failures through the same warm, specific copy pattern `KitchenClosed` already established.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`

**[P3] The finished-daily check is very tall with many independently-animated sections.**
- **Why it matters**: Header, verdict, dish facts, collapsible clue trail, stats grid, streak line, tally, occasional fan stamp, request form, then a pinned three-row footer — mitigated well by the scroll/pinned-footer split and default-collapsed trail, so this is polish, not a blocker.
- **Fix**: Worth a pass on whether the tally and streak lines can share one row on narrow screens instead of stacking.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (Confused First-Timer)**: Well served on the daily — but badly served the first time they reach `NightPage`. Nothing explains that the tile set changed from lunch, and "no spirit is a real value, not missing data" exists only as a source comment Jordan will never see.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)**: State survival is excellent (everything persists to localStorage per guess) — exactly what an interrupted mobile player needs. But the toolbar icon buttons (30px) and archive calendar day cells (34px) sit below the 44×44 comfort threshold most one-handed-thumb guidelines use, on a product that's explicitly mobile-primary. Not a WCAG 2.1 AA violation (target size is an AAA criterion there), but worth a deliberate call on whether 30px is intentional economy or an oversight.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User)**: Generally very well served — but `ResultModal` and `TabModal` have no heading element inside them (`receipt__title` / `guest-check__house` render as `<p>`, not `<h2>`), unlike `HowToModal`, `AnnouncementModal`, and `ArchiveModal`, which all use a real heading. A screen-reader user navigating by heading rotor after opening either modal gets no landmark inside the content.

## Minor Observations

- `GuessInput` has no persistent visible `<label>`, only a placeholder plus a non-visible `aria-label` — an accepted combobox pattern, but worth a second look if the two strings are ever allowed to diverge.
- `NightPage`'s "Nothing on tap" empty state doesn't play the error sound that `KitchenClosed`'s equivalent daily state does — a small cross-surface feedback-parity gap.
- `WIN_TOASTS` / `POUR_TOASTS` both fall back to index 0 on an out-of-range guess count, so a corrupted count would silently show a first-guess-win toast; low risk, just noted.
- The bar band's state-dependent subline copy ("One drink, four guesses, gone by morning" vs. "Go back and take another look") is genuinely good, on-voice microcopy.
